# 4.4 Check the health of a pod by looking at the event logs

**Troubleshooting Kubernetes Pods with** kubectl describe

A pod's startup process can encounter several issues, including:

- **Image errors:** The container image is unavailable.

- **Scheduling problems:** Worker nodes don't have enough resources or storage.

- **Configuration errors:** Typos or incorrect settings cause the pod to stop running.

**How to View Pod Events**

1.  **List pods** in the development namespace:

```bash
kubectl get pods -n development
```

2.  **Choose a pod** and copy its name.

3.  **Describe the pod** to view its details and events:

```bash
kubectl describe pod <pod-name> -n development
```

4.  **Scroll to the bottom** of the output to find the Events section.

**Understanding Pod Events**

- **Healthy pod:** Events show normal operations and successful startup.

- **Problematic pod:** Error messages help identify issues affecting the pod.

- **Long-running pod:** No recent events may appear because Kubernetes has no new issues or events to report.

**Key Takeaways**

- Most pod problems occur within the first minute of its lifecycle.

- kubectl describe pod provides detailed information about a pod.

- The Events section is useful for identifying startup and scheduling problems.

- Checking pod events is an important Kubernetes troubleshooting skill.

**Remember:** Use kubectl describe pod whenever you need to investigate why a pod isn't starting or running correctly.
